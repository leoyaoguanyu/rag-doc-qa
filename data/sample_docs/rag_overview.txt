# What is RAG?

RAG stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation. It is a technique that combines
an information retrieval step with a large language model (LLM) to answer
questions over private or domain-specific documents.

A RAG system has two main components: a retriever and a generator. The retriever
searches a document collection for passages relevant to the user's question,
typically by comparing embedding vectors with cosine similarity. The generator,
usually an LLM, then produces an answer conditioned on both the question and
the retrieved passages.

The main reason teams adopt RAG is hallucination reduction. A standalone LLM
may invent plausible-sounding but false facts when asked about documents it has
never seen. By grounding generation in retrieved context, RAG keeps answers
tied to real sources. RAG also lets teams update knowledge by re-indexing
documents, without retraining the model.
